A sick pharmacist stood in his backyard, stirring a dark syrup in an old copper kettle. He was trying to cure his own pain. He had no idea that this quiet moment was how Coca-Cola started.

His name was John Pemberton. He was a pharmacist in Atlanta, Georgia. In 1865, near the end of the American Civil War, a sword cut across his chest. The wound healed on the outside, but the pain stayed inside him. Doctors gave him morphine for the pain, and soon he could not live without it. More than money or fame, Pemberton wanted one thing: to be free of the medicine that held him.

## How Coca-Cola Started: A Drink for Five Cents

In the spring of 1886, Pemberton carried a jug of his new syrup to Jacobs' Pharmacy. There, the syrup was mixed with cold soda water and sold as a health drink. The price was five cents a glass. On the first day, he sold about nine glasses. Nine. In its first year, the drink earned about 50 dollars. Pemberton spent more than 70 dollars telling people about it.

His bookkeeper, Frank Robinson, gave the drink its name: Coca-Cola. Robinson had beautiful handwriting, and he wrote the name in soft, flowing letters. Look at a bottle today, and you can still see his handwriting.

## Why Did John Pemberton Sell His Drink?

Pemberton was still a sick man, and he needed money. In 1887 and 1888, piece by piece, he sold his drink to other businessmen. In total, he received about 2,300 dollars. On August 16, 1888, John Pemberton died, only two years after he created Coca-Cola.

The man who took the drink was Asa Candler, another pharmacist in Atlanta. Candler printed free coupons and gave them away all over the city, so people could taste one glass for free. Soon, everyone in Atlanta knew the taste.

## What This Story Teaches Us

- **Small beginnings can hide a very long story.** On his first day, Pemberton sold about nine glasses, and in the first year he spent more on adverts than the drink earned.
- **Pain can push a person to create something new.** A war wound left Pemberton in pain for years, and his search for a cure led him to the drink the whole world now knows.
- **A man can plant something he will never see grow.** Pemberton died in 1888, two years after he invented the drink, and his name was not even written on it.
